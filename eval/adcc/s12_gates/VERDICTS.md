## 1. GATE 1 — formal verdict: **NO-GO**

`hit_S=13, hit_T=12` on the core-30 (`hit_T − hit_S = −1`, required `≥ +4`); sign-test `p=1.0000` (n_effective=9, T-wins=4, S-wins=5 — no directional signal at all, consistent with the point estimate); `fp_dup_rate`: S=6.5% (2/31), T=0.0% (0/27). Both §B.4 conditions evaluated: margin condition fails outright (−1 < 4); the fp_duplicate ceiling condition independently *passes* (T is lower, not higher) but a single passing condition does not override a failing one — **verdict is NO-GO**, confirmed identical on the files_api-exclusion sensitivity check (§G.1 point 4 — the one rerouted core-30 window, none exist in this pool; §G.1's window `w37` is a straddler, outside core-30 by construction, so there is nothing to exclude and the verdict cannot be an artifact of the reroute fix).

**Scientific interpretation.** The honest conclusion is **"two-call (position+technique+validator) adds nothing over single-call at `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` on this corpus — and by the numbers, is very slightly worse."** I pulled the actual failure composition to check for confounds before writing that sentence:

- **Validator ditch behavior is NOT the driver.** Zero ditches across all 30 core windows (`gate1_predictions_arm_t.json`, verified directly). The validator's disagree-rate is a real, active 30% (9/30 `disagree`, 21/30 `agree`) — it is not rubber-stamping — but it never once withheld a real event. Arm T's `fn_breakdown` (silent=3, misclassified=15) vs. Arm S's (silent=6, misclassified=11) shows Arm T is actually MORE decisive (fewer silent misses) than Arm S, not less — it just lands on the wrong class more often when it does commit. This confound hypothesis is ruled out by direct evidence, not assumed away.
- **The real confound is axis-prompt/taxonomy calibration, and it is visible in the confusion pairs.** Pulling `(gold_class, predicted_class)` pairs for Arm T's 15 misclassifications: no single dominant failure mode, but two identifiable patterns worth flagging as follow-up material rather than dismissing: (a) `guard_pass → transition` fires 3/11 times on `guard_pass` gold — the technique axis is under-crediting real passes as "nothing happened," and since `transition` is filtered as non-scoreable before matching, these become silent losses at the scoring layer even though the model DID emit something; (b) `back_take` scatters across `submission_choke`(2)/`sweep`(2)/`guard_pass`(1)/`escape`(1)/`submission_leg_lock`(1) — 7 of 7 `back_take` gold events are wrong, with no consistent alternate label, suggesting the technique prompt's `back_take` definition ("transition into back control") is not landing cleanly at flash-lite regardless of axis-count. Neither pattern is an architecture verdict-changer at this n, but both are concrete, evidence-backed places a prompt-iteration pass (not a re-architecture) could plausibly move the number — noted for the record, not acted on here.
- **fps is fixed at 10 for both arms (§D) — not a live confound in this run**, but it means this NO-GO is scoped to fps=10 only; it says nothing about whether either arm's ranking would change at another fps.

**On `fp_dup` 6.5%→0.0%: real, but partly structural, and a hybrid idea is out of scope for this verdict.** Arm T's zero-duplicate rate is genuine (the validator-reconciled design emits at most ONE prediction per window by construction — `matcher.py`'s `fp_duplicate` literally cannot exceed 0 for a single-prediction-per-window arm on a single-gold-event window), so part of this improvement is definitional, not a discovered property of better judgment. It is still a real, legitimate design property (Arm S's open-ended "tag every exchange" call CAN and does duplicate-report within one window; Arm T structurally cannot) and is worth recording. **A single-call + validator-only reconciliation hybrid (drop the position/technique split, keep a lightweight adjudication pass to suppress Arm-S-style duplicate/hallucinated predictions) is a plausible design worth exploring — but it is a NEW architecture this run did not test, and I am not blessing it here.** It would need its own gate (own settings tuple, own subset, own margin) before any cutover decision leans on it. Flagging it as a recommended follow-up, not folding it into this verdict.

## 3. GATE 5 — formal verdict: **FLIP TO NO-GO** (with the reframe caveat attached, not silently)

Straddler Arm-T hit rate 0.0% (0/8) vs. core 40.0% (12/30) — gap 40.0pp, exceeds the 25pp flip threshold (§E.3) by a wide margin, not a borderline call.

**Caveat that MUST travel with this verdict, verbatim, to the founder:** this comparison was measured under this harness's own **untruncated** `[t_s−5s, t_s+4s]` native-video window, identical for straddlers and non-straddlers — it is **not** a measurement of production's real 720s chunk-boundary truncation effect (production clamps each highlight's analyze window to its own chunk's `[ctx.start_sec, ctx.end_sec]`, `executors.py:1286`/`:1290`; this harness never does). A 0/8 result under FULL context is evidence the 8 straddling events are simply harder cases on their own merits (longer setups, more occlusion — 1 of the 8 is itself a `submission_arm_lock`, the exact occlusion class Gate 2 already flagged as hard) — it is **not direct evidence that truncation specifically causes the loss**, since truncation was never actually applied in this measurement. **I am flipping the formal verdict to NO-GO on the numbers as specified (the design's own rule says gap>25pp flips, and it does, decisively) — but the founder brief must state that the mechanism is inferred, not directly measured, and the required design change (below) is justified independently of resolving that ambiguity, not contingent on it.**

**Required design change: add cross-chunk backward overlap to the 720s grid — recommended margin 45s.** Derivation: the two real production lookback constants that bound how far back a real highlight's setup evidence can be recovered from are `HighlightCritiqueConfig.critique_backpad_s` (max 30s — the critique pass's own backward search for a scan's true, possibly-earlier start) and `HighlightAnalyzeConfig.preroll_s` (max 15s — the analyze call's own pre-highlight context window, needed again if critique corrects the start close to a boundary). Summing the two maximums (30+15=45s) gives the smallest backward overlap that guarantees BOTH mechanisms always have their full designed lookback available even for an event whose true setup sits right at a 720s seam. This is a concrete, code-grounded number, not an arbitrary round figure — apply it as: PASS-1 scan+critique for chunk `k` gets a 45s backward-extended read into chunk `k−1`'s tail, with dedup against events chunk `k−1` already reported near the seam (the design's own §E.3 language: "add cross-chunk overlap + dedup").

**Ship as a required pre-cutover code change; verification can be lightweight, not a full re-run.** The overlap+dedup patch itself is not optional/monitoring-only — Gate 5 is a hard gate and it flipped NO-GO, so `engine#13` does not merge without this change. But *confirming the patch closes the specific gap* does not require a second full LeCun-gate cycle: once wt-s12 implements the 45s overlap, re-run just the 8 known straddling events (`GATE5_REPORT.md`'s enumerated list) through the REAL chunked+patched production pipeline and compare recall against this addendum's untruncated 0/8 baseline and the real (still-unmeasured) truncated-pre-patch baseline. That's a small, targeted regression check — appropriate to run alongside the existing gx10/Phase-5/mobile-QA release sequencing already on the roadmap, not a new blocking gate of its own.

1. **The claim under test (from the original 5-gate handoff): does Files-API regress accuracy or drop fps-honoring versus YouTube-URI?** No. `accuracy_files_api=50.0% (4/8)` vs. `accuracy_youtube=12.5% (1/8)` — no regression, and directionally favorable. fps-honoring is **CONFIRMED 2/2** (both probed clips show a strong `action_class`-level difference between fps=1 and fps=10 on the uploaded-file path) — INS-096 re-validated specifically on the production ingestion lane, closing the one item the original handoff flagged as "critically, re-confirm." Within that same skewed draw, Files-API DID score 2/4 correct on `back_take` where YouTube-URI scored 0/4 — a real, in-sample pattern (Files-API wins 4 of the 5 disagreements outright) with a physically plausible mechanism (a freshly-encoded, known-bitrate local 1080p upload vs. whatever compression/serving-quality tier Gemini's native YouTube fetch actually receives, which this project does not control or observe) — but **5 disagreement events is nowhere near enough to certify "Files-API is more accurate than YouTube-URI" as a general production claim.** I am flagging this as a plausible, follow-up-worthy hypothesis, not blessing it as a finding to act on. Arm-T spot-check (n=3, insurance-only) shows 2/3 agreement, consistent with — not contradicting — this read.

## 7. GATE 4 — formal verdict: **NOT STATS-GRADE AS-IS** (founder-decides framing, per the original gate's own text)

Read against `GATE4_REPORT.md`/`gate4_scoring.json`. Per §I.1's ruling (still binding): **no accuracy-vs-gold number exists or is claimed here** — the gold corpus carries no player-identity ground truth, full stop. Everything below is a self-consistency/honesty-channel read.

**What survived — INS-073 is structurally confirmed, and refs are load-bearing.** `sentinel_rate_without_refs=100%` (expected by schema construction — a structural check) vs. **`sentinel_rate_with_refs=0%`**: give the model ANY reference image — even the crude, unverified `ref_left`/`ref_right` half-frame crops used here — and it commits to a non-sentinel identity choice every single time (23/23). The actor axis is not decorative: reference imagery genuinely unlocks resolved identity output, exactly as INS-073 originally claimed. This is real, useful infrastructure — do not rip out the reference-image plumbing.

**What did not survive — the resolved identity is unstable, and the model gives zero honest warning of that instability.** `cross_highlight_flip_rate=62.5%` (5/8 eligible matches): in most matches, the SAME athlete is not consistently credited across that match's own multiple `successful` highlights — a serious instability signal for anything that aggregates per-player stats across a match. What makes this disqualifying rather than merely noisy: **`identity_uncertain_rate=0%`** — the model's own confidence flag never once fires, including on every one of the 5 flipping matches. A consumer of this axis today has **no signal at all**, from the model itself, distinguishing a trustworthy attribution from an unstable one. That combination — confidently wrong (or confidently inconsistent) with a zero-rate honesty channel — is the worst failure mode for the project's own "good enough to confirm/reject" bar (per project memory): a human reviewing per-player stats cannot tell which highlights to double-check, because the model never tells them to.

**A caveat that must travel with this verdict, because it cuts against over-blaming the model:** part of the 62.5% flip rate may be an artifact of THIS bootstrap's reference images, not of the underlying actor-axis mechanism. The `ref_left`/`ref_right` crops are single, early-match still-frame halves — if a match's broadcast camera pans, cuts, or reframes between highlights (routine in real ADCC footage), "left half of frame at t=3s" is not guaranteed to map to a stable spatial/visual anchor for a LATER highlight's very different camera framing, even for an otherwise-well-functioning identity model. **This design cannot currently separate "the actor axis is genuinely unstable" from "the proxy reference images are a poor visual anchor across camera changes"** — both hypotheses are consistent with the data, and only real, stable reference photographs (§I.8's stated gap) can disambiguate them.

**Plain statement for the founder: per-player stats built on the current actor-axis + proxy-reference design are NOT trustworthy for production, and should not ship as a confirm/reject-grade feature yet.**

**Design-change recommendation (my call, as asked):**
1. **Do NOT ship "surface only low-confidence outputs" as specified** — this option is dead on arrival given `identity_uncertain_rate=0%`: the model's self-reported confidence channel is not currently discriminative at all (it never fires, including on demonstrably-flipping cases), so a policy gated on it would surface nothing and provide zero real protection. Do not rely on the model's own uncertainty signal until/unless a prompt-calibration pass demonstrably makes it fire on real ambiguity.
2. **RECOMMENDED, ship-able now: per-match majority-vote / consensus reconciliation, using cross-highlight disagreement itself (which is computable post-hoc, model-agnostically) as the derived uncertainty signal** — since the model's own channel is broken, derive the signal instead of trusting it. Concretely: aggregate a match's own per-highlight actor calls, take the majority identity per `ref_id` slot, and flag the match for human review whenever the vote is not unanimous (a `disagreement` flag becomes the real "low confidence" signal, replacing the non-functional `identity_uncertain`). This directly targets the demonstrated failure mode, requires no new data collection, and matches the shape per-player stats already need (match/career aggregates, not single-clip trust).
3. **REQUIRED before broad trust, but a separate, larger effort: replace the self-referential proxy crops with a real reference-image capture flow** (real athlete photographs, or at minimum a human-verified name-to-crop labeling step) — this is the only way to close the ambiguity flagged above (genuine model instability vs. proxy-image weakness) and the only path to a true accuracy-vs-gold number in the future. Not authorized or scoped by this eval's budget; flag as a tracked follow-up, not a quick fix.

## 8. CONSOLIDATED FOUNDER BRIEF (all 5 gates — this is the text to escalate)
