## 1. The five gates at qualified settings

### Gate 1 — **NO-GO, and worse than the flash-lite record**

`hit_S=16/30 (53.3%), hit_T=11/30 (36.7%)`, margin `hit_T − hit_S = −5` (required `≥ +4`) — the flash-lite run scored `−1`; the qualified model's margin is **more than 4x wider against the two-call design**, not closer to passing. Sign-test `p=0.0625` (n_effective=5, **T-wins=0, S-wins=5** — every single disagreement in this run favored the single-call baseline, a materially more one-sided result than flash-lite's 4-5 split). `fp_dup_rate`: S=2.3% (was 6.5%), T=0.0% (unchanged) — the duplicate-suppression property holds again, but it is now the only thing left standing in Arm T's favor.

**My own falsifiable prediction (`GATES_RERUN_DESIGN.md` §8) was CONTRADICTED. Saying so plainly: I predicted `hit_T − hit_S` would improve from −1 into the 0-to-+3 range at the qualified model, reasoning that a stronger, max-thinking model should benefit MORE from a decomposed, narrower-per-call judgment task. The opposite happened — Arm S improved sharply (13→16 correct) while Arm T got WORSE (12→11 correct), widening the gap against the two-call design.** I re-pulled the confound analysis before writing this, the same discipline as v1, and it changes the story in a specific, useful way: **zero ditches again (0/30, both runs)** — validator-withholding is doubly ruled out as a cause, at both model tiers. But the misclassification pattern **consolidated** rather than improved: at flash-lite, `back_take` failures were scattered across many different wrong labels; at the qualified model, a single dominant, systematic bias emerged — **`back_take → submission_choke` (4 of 7 back_take windows, both arms)** and, newly, **`guard_pass → submission_choke` (4 of 11 guard_pass windows, Arm T)** — the model has developed a confident, reproducible over-calling of `submission_choke` on adjacent categories, rather than reducing its error rate. **The honest conclusion: more thinking did not fix the calibration problem — it changed its SHAPE, from diffuse uncertainty to a concentrated, wrong prior.** That is a materially different and more useful finding than "the model is still just bad at this," and it argues against "give it a stronger model" as a fix for this specific failure mode at all — the axis-prompt's `back_take`/`submission_choke` boundary definition itself is implicated, independent of model tier.

### Gate 2 — **NO-GO, and the finding strengthened from "no gain" to "active regression"**

`accuracy_low=75.0% (12/16), accuracy_high=68.8% (11/16)`, `net_delta=−1` (required `≥+2`) — flash-lite scored a flat `net_delta=0` (tie); the qualified model shows HIGH resolution **actively making the validator worse**, not merely failing to help. Mechanism, pinned to a single clip: window `cfd4` was correct at LOW (`agree` → `submission_arm_lock`, gold `submission_arm_lock`) and wrong at HIGH (`disagree` → overturned to `submission_leg_lock`) — **HIGH resolution triggered the validator to second-guess and overturn a correct answer.** My prediction that the arm_lock↔leg_lock confusion would "reduce but not vanish, at least 2 of 8 still misclassify" is **confirmed and exceeded**: at LOW, exactly 2/8 arm_lock clips misattribute to `submission_leg_lock` (my predicted floor, hit precisely); at HIGH, it's **4/8** — worse. My prediction that "Gate 2's core HIGH-vs-LOW finding is more likely than not to persist" is **confirmed, in a stronger form than predicted** (I predicted persistence of a null result; the qualified-model data shows an actual regression). Sharper characterization now available with the class-level split: **`submission_leg_lock` is fully solved (8/8 correct at both resolutions) — 100% of the remaining error is concentrated in `submission_arm_lock`, and specifically in the one-way `arm_lock→leg_lock` direction** (never the reverse). This is not a visibility problem at either resolution — it's a one-directional prior the model holds regardless of pixel count, and regardless of model tier.

**Design recommendation unchanged and now on stronger evidence: drop `ValidatorAxisConfig.media_resolution="high"` before any cutover.** Not "no justification exists" (the v1 framing) — now "actively net negative, with an identified single-clip mechanism (over-eager validator second-guessing)."

### Gate 3 — **PASS-WITH-FINDING confirmed, delta shrank as predicted; fps-honoring now an open question**

`agreement_rate=50.0% (4/8, up from 37.5%)`, `accuracy_youtube=37.5% (3/8, up from 12.5%)`, `accuracy_files_api=50.0% (4/8, unchanged)`, `accuracy_delta=+12.5pp` (down sharply from `+37.5pp`). **This is exactly what I flagged as the likely explanation in `VERDICTS.md` §6: the flash-lite `+37.5pp` delta was driven by an anomalously low, unstratified-draw YouTube-URI baseline (12.5%, versus Gate 1's true ~43% core rate), not a real Files-API accuracy advantage.** At the qualified model, `accuracy_youtube` rose to a more representative 37.5% and the "advantage" shrank by two-thirds — consistent with my "do not over-claim, likely partly noise" ruling being correct. **Verdict stands: PASS on the production-blocking question (no regression — files_api 50% ≥ youtube 37.5%).** New wrinkle this run: **INS-096 fps-honoring did NOT reconfirm (0/2, down from 2/2 at flash-lite)** — both probed clips returned the identical `action_class` at fps=1 and fps=10 on the Files-API path. At n=2 this cannot be read as "fps is being silently ignored" (a real infrastructure regression) versus "the qualified model is simply more stable/less fps-sensitive on these two particular clips" (a benign explanation) — **flagging this as an open, unresolved item requiring a slightly larger, better-chosen fps-honoring probe (e.g. a fast-scramble clip where fps sensitivity is more likely to matter) before treating INS-096 as closed on the qualified-model Files-API path.** Not a blocker — Gate 3's PASS does not depend on it — but not silently dropped either.

### Gate 4 — **improved, still NOT stats-grade — re-rendered**

`sentinel_rate_with_refs=0%` / `sentinel_rate_without_refs=100%` — INS-073 reconfirmed again (refs remain load-bearing, unchanged from flash-lite). `identity_uncertain_rate=0%` — **unchanged, still zero.** `cross_highlight_flip_rate=37.5% (3/8, down from 62.5%)` — a real, substantial improvement in attribution stability at the qualified model. **Re-rendered stats-grade ruling: still NOT stats-grade for unattended production trust, but meaningfully closer.** The reasoning: a 37.5% flip rate is a real improvement (the model is more internally consistent within a match than it was), but the disqualifying combination from v1 is **unchanged in kind**: `identity_uncertain_rate=0%` means the model still gives **zero honest warning** on any of the 3 matches that do flip — a human reviewer still has no model-provided signal telling them which attributions to double-check. Improved accuracy does not substitute for a working confidence channel. **Majority-vote recommendation confirmed at the qualified model, unchanged:** ship per-match majority-vote/consensus reconciliation using cross-highlight disagreement (still computable, still the only honest derived-uncertainty signal, since `identity_uncertain` is still non-functional) as the actual review-trigger — now flagging only 3 of 8 matches for review instead of 5, a real, direct improvement in the SAME design's practical cost the moment it ships. Real reference-photo capture remains the required, separately-scoped long-term fix (§I.8/`VERDICTS.md` §7 gap is unchanged — this run reused the same proxy crops, and the ambiguity between "model instability" and "proxy-image weakness" is not resolved by this run).

### Gate 5 — **re-rendered: mechanical NO-FLIP this run, required fix ships anyway**

`straddler Arm-T hit rate = 62.5% (5/8)` vs. `non-straddler core = 36.7% (11/30)` — gap `(core − straddler) = −25.8pp`. This is a **complete reversal** from flash-lite's `0.0% vs 40.0%` (gap `+40.0pp`, which flipped). At the qualified model, straddlers do **better** than the core, not worse, so the design's own flip condition (core underperforming straddlers by >25pp in the "straddlers are harder" direction) does not trigger — mechanically, **NO FLIP** this run.

**Formal ruling: I am NOT reading this reversal as vindication that straddling events are fine.** A swing from 0/8 to 5/8 on the identical 8 events (only the model changed) means this specific measurement — accuracy on n=8 under full, untruncated context — has now produced two wildly different, mutually contradictory directional signals across two model generations. At n=8, a 2-3 event shift swings the result by 25+ points either way; **this methodology has proven itself too noise-dominated to support a confident directional claim in either direction, and neither v1's "straddlers are catastrophic" nor v2's "straddlers are fine" should be trusted as a characterization of real straddler difficulty.** Both runs share the same underlying limitation restated from v1: this addendum measures difficulty under FULL, untruncated context — it has never once measured the actual production truncation effect, in either run.

**Given that, the required design change ships regardless of this run's mechanical no-flip**: the 45s cross-chunk overlap + dedup fix (`critique_backpad_s` 30s max + `preroll_s` 15s max) was never justified by the accuracy addendum alone — it is justified by first-principles chunking-boundary reasoning (production's real 720s zero-overlap grid genuinely can lose setup evidence across a seam, a structural fact independent of any model) plus the nonzero, if small, corpus-wide straddle count (1.4%, 8/557 at the decision-relevant W=60). **Ship the fix as a low-cost robustness/hygiene improvement, not because the noisy addendum currently points one way — it has pointed both ways, and neither is reliable enough to be the deciding evidence.** Verification remains the same lightweight post-fix regression check specified in `VERDICTS.md` §3, not a third full addendum re-run.

### 9.1 The headline anomaly: the architecture question is model-dependent, not task-dependent

**This is the real finding, and it overturns an implicit assumption every prior gate in this program shared.** Gate 1 measured "does the two/three-call split beat single-call" as if it were a property of the TASK (BJJ highlight technique-tagging). It is not — it is at least partly a property of the MODEL. Gemini: single-call (53.3%/51.7%) dominates the triple (36.7%/25.0%) by a wide, reproduced margin, at BOTH model tiers tested. Kimi: the triple (41.7%) beats its own single-call (20.7%) by an even wider margin, in the opposite direction. Neither result is noise-level — both gaps are large (≈15-30pp) on their respective n.

**Three hypotheses, not mutually exclusive, stated with what would discriminate between them — none of this was run, this is proposal only:**

**(a) Schema/format complexity vs. instruction-following profile.** Arm S's single-call schema is open-ended and compound: the model must self-segment the window into however many discrete exchanges it judges occurred, then fill 8 fields per segment (`start_s`/`end_s`/`position`/`actor`/`actor_gi_color`/`action_class`/`outcome`/`specific_technique_guess`/`confidence`) inside one JSON array of unknown length. Arm T's three calls are each narrow and closed: ONE enum + one justification sentence (position); TWO enums + one justification (technique); a bounded reconciliation object (validator) — no self-directed segmentation, no open list cardinality, ever. Gemini's own `simplified-tags-time-v1` schema is also the ONE format this entire multi-year project has iterated on most heavily (the INS-096/097/102/103 fps/window-tuning lineage this whole gate program grew out of) — it is plausible Gemini's strong Arm-S showing reflects genuine, project-specific schema familiarity/tuning as much as raw capability. Kimi has had none of that iteration. **This hypothesis predicts**: Kimi's weakness is specifically about OPEN-ENDED, SELF-SEGMENTING, multi-item extraction — not about complex schemas or multiple fields per se.

**(b) Kimi's always-on reasoning benefits from decomposition.** The §9.1 probe confirmed Kimi K3 "always reasons" — `reasoning_effort` cannot be disabled, only tuned (`low`/`high`/`max`). A single, narrow question ("what position, and why") gives that reasoning process one clean target to deliberate toward. A compound task (segment this clip into N exchanges, then simultaneously judge 8 fields per exchange) forces the SAME reasoning budget to juggle multiple concurrent sub-judgments inside one generation pass before emitting a large structured array — plausibly diluting or confusing an architecture whose strength is depth-per-question, not breadth-per-turn. Gemini's `thinking` is a tunable, optional overlay on a base model already tuned for structured extraction; Kimi's reasoning is load-bearing and constant. **This hypothesis predicts**: Kimi's weakness tracks the NUMBER OF CONCURRENT JUDGMENTS demanded per call, independent of whether they're wrapped in a list.

**(c) Video-tokenization density / attention dilution.** Kimi's native video tokenizes far more densely than Gemini's for the identical 9s clip — ~31K prompt tokens vs. Gemini's own measured ~6.6-8.2K per axis call on the same window (real ledger numbers, not estimated). A ~4x-longer token sequence could make coherent multi-item, multi-field extraction (Arm S's shape) structurally harder — more content to hold in coherent working attention while deciding how many events occurred and filling many fields — while a SINGLE bounded judgment at the end of that same long sequence is comparatively unaffected, since there's only one thing to extract regardless of sequence length. **This hypothesis predicts**: the effect should scale with clip/token length — a SHORTER window would narrow the single-call-vs-triple gap for Kimi if this is the dominant mechanism, and would NOT if (a) or (b) dominate instead.

**The discriminating experiment** (proposal only, not run): a **single-item, multi-field, pre-bounded-window** variant of Arm S's schema on Kimi-native — same window, same 8 fields, but return ONE object instead of an array (no self-directed segmentation) — staged against a **single-item, few-field** variant (just `position`+`action_class`+`outcome`, no justification-heavy fields, still one call, still bounded). If Kimi scores near its 41.7% triple-level on the single-item/multi-field variant, that isolates OPEN-ENDED SEGMENTATION specifically as the culprit (favors hypothesis a — schema/instruction-following mismatch on list generation, not field count or reasoning-decomposition per se). If it instead scores near its 20.7% single-call floor even without the list requirement, that points toward field-count/reasoning-dilution (hypothesis b) or token-density (hypothesis c) — and a SECOND, follow-up variant holding field-count fixed while shortening the window would then separate (b) from (c). A cheap, staged, small-n (n=8-10 per variant) design — not authorized or run here, named because the CEO asked what WOULD discriminate, not because it's recommended as the next spend (see §9.4 for what I'd actually recommend spending on).

### 9.2 Does this change the production recommendation? **No — confirming the CEO's read, with the numbers made explicit.**

Kimi's BEST-performing configuration (the triple, 41.7%) is still well below Gemini's WORST-performing configuration measured in this program that anyone would actually ship (Arm S, 51.7-53.3%) — and the cost gap is not a rounding error. Real, ledger-verified per-call costs at the qualified-model tier: Gemini Arm S averages **$0.00631/call**; Kimi-native primary averages **$0.146/call** — **≈23x more expensive, for less than half the accuracy (20.7% vs 51.7%).** Per-window triple-vs-triple: Gemini's own Arm T triple (position+technique+validator) averages **$0.02519/window**; Kimi-native's triple averages **$0.4369/window** — **≈17x more expensive**, and while Kimi's triple DOES beat Gemini's own triple on accuracy (41.7% vs 25.0%), it still loses to Gemini's cheap, unglamorous single call. **There is no cell in this 2-model × 2-architecture matrix where Kimi beats Gemini's Arm S on cost, accuracy, or both.** Gemini Arm S remains strictly dominant — cheapest AND most accurate — among every combination measured in this entire campaign. **Confirmed, not dissented: nothing here changes the standing recommendation (single-call `gemini-3.5-flash` + the three required design changes already ruled).**

### 9.4 Follow-up options for the founder — named, costed honestly, none required

This idea would need its own small, dedicated pilot (measuring Kimi-triple accuracy specifically on Gemini-disagreement windows) before it's more than a hypothesis — naming it because the CEO asked, not because I'm recommending it be built.

## 10. AC7 — Gate 5 overlap-fix verification (production `engine#13` re-scope) — THE CLOSING MEASUREMENT

Author: Yann LeCun | Read against `GATE5_VERIFICATION_REPORT.md`/`gate5_verification_scoring.json` directly. This is the last open measurement of the entire campaign — real, unmodified `wt-s12` production code (`_outer_chunks`, `highlight_critique_node`/`highlight_analyze_node`, `seam_dedup.dedup_match_clips`), the actual shipped 45s-overlap patch, tested against the same 8 straddler events Gate 5 has now measured three times.

**Headline:** patched (45s overlap + dedup) **1/8 (12.5%)**; pre-patch, zero-overlap, never measured before **0/8 (0.0%)**; untruncated references: flash-lite 0/8, qualified-model 5/8 (62.5%).

### 10.1 (a) The fix is strictly better than zero-overlap, and cheap — confirmed, unambiguous

`1/8 > 0/8` is a real, positive, directionally-correct effect, and it is the FIRST time the "does truncation actually cost recall" question has an actual measured pre-patch baseline to compare against — every prior Gate-5 addendum (v1 and v2) measured untruncated difficulty only and explicitly could not speak to truncation itself (`VERDICTS.md` §3, `VERDICTS_V2.md` §1). **This closes that specific, long-standing gap: yes, zero-overlap chunking does cost real recall on straddling events (0/8), and the 45s patch does recover some of it (1/8).** The mechanism is confirmed structurally, not just statistically — event e3's chunk-1 boundary sits at exactly the straddle point (t_s=761 vs. the 720s grid line), and the patch's 45s backward reach is confirmed sufficient to pull the missing setup evidence into view for that one event. The patch costs nothing incremental to ship (a few extra seconds of re-processed video per chunk boundary, not a new call architecture) — there is no scenario in which shipping it is worse than not shipping it.

**The seam-dedup mechanism is a second, independently clean positive finding, separate from the recall discussion.** 7/8 events carried genuine double-publish risk under the stress test; dedup correctly merged 3/7 (including a real cross-submission-family compatibility case, e1) and correctly DECLINED to merge the other 4/7 — not a defect, but the conservative-by-design behavior working exactly as documented (it cannot safely merge two class-incompatible labels without risking silently discarding a real, different event). **Both shipped mechanisms — overlap and dedup — behave exactly as designed, with real evidence, not just code inspection.**

### 10.2 (b) Chunk-clamping still costs most of the recoverable events — structural ceiling, ACCEPT, do not chase with more chunking engineering

**1/8 vs. the untruncated qualified-model ceiling of 5/8 means the patch recovers only 1 of the 4 theoretically-recoverable events (25% of the gap) — chunk-boundary clamping, even with the 45s reach, remains the dominant suppressor on this small sample.** That is a real, disclosed limitation and the founder should read it as exactly that, not as "the fix didn't work" (§10.1 already establishes that it did, partially).

**But I do not read this as forcing another chunking-architecture design change before deploy, for a specific, evidence-grounded reason: at least part of the remaining gap is very plausibly a MODEL-CALIBRATION problem this program has already identified and already addressed with a DIFFERENT fix, not a pure chunking-boundary problem more overlap would solve.** Look at the per-event table: 4 of the 8 straddlers are `back_take` gold events (e3, e6, e7, e8) — and `back_take` is the exact class `VERDICTS_V2.md` §1 already flagged as the qualified model's single most systematic failure mode (a reproduced `back_take→submission_choke` bias, 4/7 in the Gate-1 core-30, present regardless of chunking). Only e3 flips; e6/e7/e8 stay wrong under BOTH configs, and their wrong answers (`guard_pass`, `escape`, `guard_pass`) don't even cleanly match that specific bias — consistent with a model that struggles with `back_take` broadly, independent of how much context it's given. **I cannot cleanly separate, from this data alone, how much of the residual 4-event gap is "truncation-recoverable-with-more-overlap" versus "the model would get this wrong even with full context, for calibration reasons unrelated to chunking"** — the untruncated ceiling being 5/8 rather than 8/8 confirms the model itself isn't perfect on these events even without any chunking constraint at all. Given that ambiguity, **spending further chunking-architecture effort (extending the overlap window further, or restructuring the 720s grid) risks chasing a problem that a bigger overlap window cannot fully solve, when the already-shipped single-call fallback (Gate 1's own required fix) is the more direct lever for the calibration half of this gap.** `MAX_SCOPE_SEC=720` is accepted as a hard constraint here, not revisited — the evidence doesn't clearly justify challenging it, and challenging it without that evidence would be exactly the kind of unearned architecture change this whole program has tried to avoid.

### 10.3 (c) Corpus-wide residual — ship with monitoring, explicit trigger stated

**Corpus-wide expected impact: ≈4/557 ≈ 0.7% additional events where full-context accuracy would have caught something the patched, chunked production pipeline still misses** (the gap between the untruncated ceiling and the patched result, scaled by the straddler exposure rate of 8/557 ≈ 1.4%). This is small — smaller than several other residuals this campaign has already accepted with monitoring (Gate 3's fps-honoring open question, Gate 4's proxy-reference-image ambiguity) — and it is now backed by a REAL measured pre/post-patch delta, not an inferred or assumed one. **Ruling: ACCEPTABLE TO SHIP WITH MONITORING.**

**Concrete post-deploy trigger, not a vague "watch it":** in production's existing human-in-the-loop audit/correction data (already collected per this project's standing async-correction workflow), track the correction/miss rate specifically for highlights whose event timestamp falls within 45-90s of a 720s chunk boundary (i.e., straddler-adjacent events — near enough to plausibly be affected by the same boundary mechanics, whether or not they technically straddle) against the corpus's general correction/miss rate. **If straddler-adjacent events show a correction rate more than 2x the general baseline, sustained over ≥20 straddler-adjacent production events (large enough to not be a repeat of this report's own n=8 noise problem), that is the trigger to revisit** — at that point, re-open the overlap-margin question with real production-scale data instead of this program's necessarily small, targeted samples.

### 10.4 (d) Discovery-stage bypass — a real, disclosed scope caveat, not a defect

This verification tested the SECOND half of the production chain (critique + analyze, real chunk-scope window-clamping) with a hand-constructed "found highlight" input (the same W=60/post_tail_s=5 span that already defines these 8 events as straddlers — not fabricated, but not independently re-derived either) — it explicitly did **not** exercise the real PASS-1 `highlight_scan` discovery stage, disclosed for a real, understandable reason (unbounded per-chunk call cost, not corner-cutting). **This means AC7 verifies "does the chunk-scope clamp lose evidence once a highlight is already found near a boundary" — it does NOT verify "does the real discovery stage even find/bound a straddling highlight correctly in the first place near a truncated boundary."** That second link in the chain remains unverified. I am not treating this as blocking the verdict below (the mechanism this check DID verify is the one `GATE5_ADDENDUM.md`/`VERDICTS.md` §3/`VERDICTS_V2.md` §1 explicitly identified as the truncation site, and it is now real-code-verified, not inferred) — but it is a genuine, named gap, not a footnote to bury: **a full discovery-stage straddler probe is a legitimate, low-priority follow-up, not a re-open of this verdict.**

### 10.5 Formal AC7 verdict: **VERIFIED-WITH-ACCEPTED-RESIDUAL**

Not **VERIFIED** outright — 1/8 is too weak an absolute number, and too far below the untruncated ceiling, to call this problem closed. Not **NOT-VERIFIED (design change required)** — the fix is real, positive, cheap, structurally confirmed, and the residual is small (0.7% corpus-wide), plausibly partly attributable to an already-separately-addressed model-calibration issue rather than purely a chunking defect, and comes with a concrete, actionable monitoring trigger rather than an open-ended "keep an eye on it." **Ship the 45s overlap + seam-dedup patch as-is. Accept the residual with the §10.3 monitoring trigger. Do not further engineer the chunking architecture on this evidence alone. File the discovery-stage gap (§10.4) as a named, low-priority follow-up.**
