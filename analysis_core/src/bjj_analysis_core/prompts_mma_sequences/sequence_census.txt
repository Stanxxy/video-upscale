List every technique sequence in this short observation window of an MMA fight, round or sparring footage. Inspect the supplied video frame by frame and use the attached fighter crops to tell the fighters apart.

A sequence is one fighter's unbroken chain of techniques: each technique starts no more than 1.5 seconds after the previous one ends. A longer pause, a reset to distance with nothing thrown, a referee break or a round break ends the sequence. Techniques of any class chain together: jab, jab, cross is one sequence; jab, cross into a double leg is one sequence; a single leg that lands followed at once by ground punches is one sequence. A single technique on its own is a sequence of one step. The opponent's counters are the opponent's own sequences, even while they overlap in time. Report every sequence with at least one step visible in the window, and give only the steps you can see inside the window.

Who attacks. For every sequence first write a one-line summary and actor_appearance, what the attacking fighter looks like in this footage (shorts colour, skin, hair, tattoos), and only then choose actor_player_id: the ordered_players entry whose fighter crop matches that appearance. Use the fighters' display names, never their ids, in summary and evidence. The attacker is the fighter whose fist, elbow, knee, shin or foot travels toward the opponent. Blocking, parrying, catching a kick, covering up, raising a guard, framing, pushing away and slipping are defence: never list them as techniques, and never give the defender a strike because the attacker's strike touched their arms. When both fighters throw at the same moment, each strike is its own entry for the fighter who threw it.

Count every technique. Watch each fighter's two hands and two legs separately. In a fast exchange two punches can land within 0.3 seconds of each other (a jab then a hook, a left hook then a right overhand); list every one as its own step, in order, with its own start and end. A kick or knee thrown straight after punches is its own step. Never merge two techniques into one step because they are close together.

Each step is one technique with its action class from action_classes and, for classes listed in technique_names, its specific technique name from that list. For a class without a list (sweep, guard_pass, back_take, escape, transition) set technique to null and describe the move in note. Use technique other only with a note that names the move. Strikes are strike_punch, strike_kick, strike_knee, strike_elbow, and ground_strike for any strike thrown while either fighter is on the ground. Consecutive identical ground strikes by the same fighter (a burst of ground-and-pound) may be one ground_strike step with repeat = how many were thrown and landed = how many landed; every other step has repeat 1, and landed is given only for ground_strike steps (0 to repeat).

Technique names. Punches: jab is the lead hand thrown straight; cross is the rear hand thrown straight down the centre; hook is a bent-arm punch travelling sideways (either hand); overhand is the rear hand looping over the top on a downward arc; uppercut travels upward. Kicks: roundhouse turns the hip over and hits with the shin or instep (a roundhouse to the leg is a low kick: roundhouse with strike_target leg; calf_kick only when it targets the calf below the knee); teep pushes straight forward with the sole; side_kick is thrown with the body turned sideways, driving the heel out; oblique_kick stomps at the knee. A knee in the clinch is clinch_knee.

Tell striking from grappling by what the hands and hips do, not by arm extension alone. Lowering the level, shooting at or grabbing the legs, hips or body, or driving through the opponent is a takedown_attempt (double_leg, single_leg, body_lock, trip, throw), never a punch, even when the arms reach forward. Tying up the head, arms or body is a clinch_entry. A strike thrown just before a shot is its own step before the takedown step. Feints, pawing that never extends, pushes, frames, grip fighting, stance switches and footwork are not techniques.

Timing in absolute source-video integer milliseconds, inside the window (the window's start_ms and end_ms are absolute times): start_ms is when the technique begins (the throw or the shot starts); for a strike, end_ms is the moment of contact, or of full extension when it misses; for any other technique, end_ms is when its immediate result can be seen (control settled, defended, escaped or abandoned). When the window ends before a step's result, end it at the window end with outcome unclear. Order steps by start_ms.

For each step first write short visual evidence of what you see, then classify it. Outcome per step: a strike is successful when it lands on the opponent's head, body or legs, even when partly blocked; it is unsuccessful when it hits only the guard (gloves or forearms), is slipped, or hits air; unclear when contact cannot be seen. Any other technique is successful when it achieved its goal and unsuccessful when it was defended. For strike steps give strike_target (head, body or leg: where it landed or was aimed), significant (true for every strike thrown at striking range and for power strikes in the clinch or on the ground; false for light pawing or pitty-pat strikes) and knockdown (true only when this strike itself drops the opponent or takes their footing); for every other step set strike_target and significant to null and knockdown to false.

Only live action counts: if the window shows a broadcast replay, slow motion, a round break, walkouts, the referee holding the fighters apart, or the decision, return no sequences for that footage. Echo the supplied window_index. Return an empty sequences array only when no technique is thrown. Return only the required JSON schema.
